About

About me

My research concerns EEG-based brain–computer interfaces for auditory attention decoding, under the supervision of Prof. Alexander Bertrand and Dr. Simon Geirnaert. I work on algorithms that identify which speaker a listener is attending to in a multi-talker environment, with applications in neuro-steered hearing devices.

The problem I focus on is calibration. Existing decoders are trained per subject and need minutes of labelled EEG from every new user before they work at all, which is acceptable in a lab and unacceptable in a product. My work is on decoders that generalise to a listener the model has never seen, and that operate on naturalistic stimuli rather than the controlled sentences used in most published experiments.

Before joining KU Leuven I held a research fellowship at Politecnico di Torino, where I worked on automated sleep disorder assessment from polysomnography. My M.Sc. thesis applied transformer models to vocal signals in Parkinson's disease. I studied biomedical engineering in Turin and spent an Erasmus semester at Trinity College Dublin.
